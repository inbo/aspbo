Frequently Asked Questions

Functionalities

Report missing data

It is possible to report missing data via the ‘contact’ button. This button can be found in the bottom left corner of the page.

Download data

It is possible to download the data behind each table and graph via the ‘download data’ button. This button can be found in the links below most graphs, tables or maps.

Export graphs

It is possible to export charts and maps as images. This can be done by clicking the ‘download graph’ button, which is located at the bottom left of the chart.
Charts can also be exported as images by hovering your mouse over the chart and clicking the ‘camera’ icon. This icon is located at the top right of the chart.

Export maps

It is possible to export maps as images. This can be done by clicking on the ‘download map’ button. This button can be found in the bottom left corner of the map.

Share link

It is possible to share a link to the species information of the chosen species (including filters). This can be done by clicking on the ‘share link’ button. This button can be found in the top right corner of the page.

Download PDF report

It is possible to download a PDF report of the species information of the selected species. This report includes all species-specific graphs, tables and maps and their associated settings. This report can be created by clicking on the ‘create report’ button. This button can be found in the bottom left corner of the page.

Additional information

What is TrIAS?

TrIAS brings together the expertise and knowledge of nine ongoing and past BELSPO projects: Alien Alert, Invaxen, Diars, INPLANBEL, Alien Impact, Ensis, CORDEX.be, Speedy and the Belgian Biodiversity Platform. The project is based on two components: 1) building a data network around invasive species and 2) developing data-driven procedures for risk analysis based on modeling, mapping and risk assessment. A crucial objective of TrIAS is to predict the invasion risk of alien species in response to climate change through automated procedures. Correlative species distribution models (SDMs) are used to create risk maps under current climate and different future emission scenarios.
